I.

On building

I like problems where the data is messy, the users are real and someone has to decide what to do next.

I grew up in Jabalpur, Madhya Pradesh, and went to IIT Bombay to study energy. Somewhere between the labs and the hostel I found I liked building things even more than studying them. In my first year I taught myself full-stack development and hand-coded my first personal website, line by line, just to see if I could.

Then came Voom Cabs, a cab fare comparison startup that won a pre-incubation grant on campus, and a string of internships that pulled me toward business: the CEO's office at MCaffeine, growth advisory at Frost & Sullivan, credit automation at Evam Finance. I also ran internship outreach for my department through the Placement Office.

Today I work on content discovery and churn at Meesho, and at night I build things like Earth Stats and Godly. I also build small tools just for myself, like an agent that emails me the morning's energy news and an app that plans my money. The long game is a company of my own, ideally one that fixes something broken in how the world makes and uses energy.

III.

Field notes

Four years of energy at IIT Bombay, and a habit of explaining it to anyone who'll listen.

The courses I loved most were the hands-on ones: designing real energy systems, testing renewables in the lab, and modelling how energy moves through a system. Alongside them I took entrepreneurship, design thinking, economics and energy policy, plus coursework in data science and machine learning.

Now I write about the newest energy research and technology on LinkedIn, in plain language for people who don't work in the field. It keeps me close to the problems I want to build around.

I also keep a research notebook on energy startups: accelerators around the world, robots that build solar farms, and how power gets to data centres.

Voom Cabs

Startup, founding team, 2025 – 26. Closed

One screen comparing fares and arrival times across India's cab apps. Won a pre-incubation grant from IIT Bombay's entrepreneurship school. We wound it down in 2026: the product depended on data the cab platforms don't openly share, and the policy and feasibility hurdles outweighed what we could build around them. The lesson I kept: check what your product depends on before you build on it.
